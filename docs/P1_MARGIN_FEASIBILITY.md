# P1 isolated margin feasibility

**Defer margin for the hackathon.** The imported engine has useful position sizing, swap settlement and recovery code. The deployed P0 contracts cannot attach it through configuration. A long-only experiment needs a new account and controller version, a funded settlement route in both directions and independent close and liquidation evidence. Keep the P0 lending demo as the submission path. Building P1 requires end-to-end P0 acceptance and an explicit decision by Claude and the founder. The design below was reviewed at repository commit `329637f13a7a77135d04f0e46485bb32612203fb`. P1 remains design-only.

## What the import actually does

The imported Peridot source is pinned at `e08e53632fbc3a5f08f7f4d14f45b1ea90e4b2e3`. Its isolated long takes quote-asset pTokens as margin. The quote asset and debt asset must match. Each position gets a custody clone, separate from its owner. The executor redeems the starting margin, draws an ERC-3156 flash loan, swaps the combined quote balance into the base asset, mints base pTokens to the clone, and borrows principal plus the flash fee from the debt book. The clone owes the lending debt. The executor settles the flash loan and then checks actual equity, caps and requested leverage. The swap module measures token balance changes, checks the configured router, minimum received and oracle deviation, and clears its allowance. [Executor](../contracts/src/core/margin/IsolatedMarginExecutorUpgradeable.sol) · [Account and custody](../contracts/src/core/margin/IsolatedMarginAccount.sol) · [Swap module](../contracts/src/core/margin/IsolatedMarginSwapModule.sol) · [Margin math](../contracts/src/core/margin/IsolatedMarginMath.sol)

The legacy risk engine calls `registerIsolatedMarginAccount` on its controller. The legacy controller dispatches registered borrowers to a separate risk hook and excludes them from ordinary pToken liquidation and seizure. Its specialized liquidator has a public flash-funded route and an allowlisted, funded in-kind fallback. The engine sizes recovery using equity and maintenance margin, and records status in its own position ledger. An ordinary P0 liquidator instead brings debt tokens and receives collateral pTokens under the active controller's lending LTV and close factor. Combining both liquidation authorities would leave competing seizure rules and position accounting. ## Smallest worthwhile experiment after a separate go decision

Use one pair, one open long per dedicated Sentinel margin account, an owner-initiated open and full close, and one collateral/debt book pair per isolated market. As an illustrative test cap, use up to 1.5× gross exposure. Exclude shorts, partial closes, position increases, automation grants for opening, generic router calldata, fee distribution, TP/SL orders and cross-market collateral. Keep the margin account itself as the actual pToken borrower, with a separate balance from the user's existing P0 lending account. A new factory can bind the account, policy owner, market and fixed executor at creation. Reuse the imported quoter's conservative sizing, flash callback checks, swap delta checks and post-fill math. This estimate covers the long-only adaptation.

1. **Open.** The margin owner supplies quote equity. Accrue the books and check the approved pair, fresh positive independent prices, open flag, raw caps, leverage limit, fees and minimum output. An isolated flash source advances the quote asset. Swap equity plus flash principal once into the collateral asset, credit the account's measured output, enter collateral and mint the collateral shares. Keep the ordinary mint supply-cap and eligible-policy checks. Bind the flash callback to lender, initiator, account, token, amount and exact operation. Bind the route, assets, recipients, deadline, min-out and maximum fee at the typed entry point.
2. **Authorize debt at the book.** The authenticated `borrowAllowed` path must recognize only the registered margin account's one pending open, for this market, debt book and exact debt increment. Compute the debt including the flash fee and the received collateral using actual accrued books, decimals and oracle values. Enforce active market, capacity, minimum remaining cash, hard LTV and margin limits. Encode the same context for the MARKET and ACCOUNT adapter calls with `op=6`: fresh flags, post-operation LTV rounded up in bps, and the reserve check. Both must allow. Finalize the flash repayment and validate actual settled equity, leverage and debt before marking the position open. Any failure reverts the whole transaction. Direct pToken calls outside this authenticated path retain ordinary controls. Neither a user-provided flag nor a special router address alone can grant margin privileges.
3. **Version the policy semantics.** Require the protocol cash floor in Solidity on every new borrow, including an open. If the market's advertised reserve toggle applies to op 6, publish a new market template and Studio commitment that cover BORROW and MARGIN_OPEN. Keep the existing compiler, circuits and commitments reproducible. Select a registered account circuit whose allowed-action list includes 6. Store the actual layered outcomes in successful receipts and use decoded revert data for rejected calls.
4. **Full close.** Accrue and repay the entire debt first, from owner/account funds or from separately funded flash liquidity. Verify zero raw debt before releasing posted collateral. For a swap-assisted close, sell sufficient collateral through the pinned route to cover principal, flash fee and bounded costs. Return residue as idle underlying to the account/owner. Provide an owner-funded repay followed by an in-kind debt-free exit when the DEX, flash liquidity or feeds are unavailable. Neither `MARGIN_CLOSE` nor repayment invokes a circuit. Cash still limits redemptions. A debt-free owner can instead receive its pToken shares.
5. **Liquidation.** For this strictly two-book design, use the P0 permissionless funded lending liquidation as the sole seizure path. The liquidator repays debt with its own quote tokens and receives bounded collateral pTokens in kind. Positions derive remaining collateral and debt from the actual books after a partial liquidation. Align the displayed maintenance boundary to the controller's strict liquidation-LTV comparison, including rounding at equality. The P1 account's liquidation and settled-closure views must reconcile after every seizure. Do not install the imported special liquidator alongside this rule. A proposal to keep the original engine's maintenance sizing, insurance or vault ledger instead requires its separate liquidation authority, status transitions and a new estimate.

**Exit order matters.** The imported flash close repays first, redeems collateral, swaps and can remint leftover quote into pTokens. The imported liquidator may also remint residual user or insurance funds. P0 `mintAllowed` applies a discretionary policy on every mint, including a debt-free mint. P0 `redeemAllowed` and `transferAllowed` inspect outstanding debt before escrowed collateral moves. Simply bypassing circuits on the outer close or liquidation call would leave nested dependencies. The proposed full-close and in-kind liquidation paths avoid residual mints and avoid a blanket exemption for redeems or transfers. **Illustrative sizing only.** With 100 mock USD of equity, 50 mock USD of flash principal, a 0.025 fee and a 1% execution haircut, the account would hold collateral valued at 148.50 and owe 50.025. Its rounded-up LTV would be 3,369 bps. Actual equity would be 98.475, so gross leverage would exceed a 1.5× maximum. The resulting leverage is about 1.508×. For a 5-bps flash fee and this same haircut, the pre-rounding principal limit is 99 ÷ 2.0115, approximately 49.217 mock USD. The quoter must round down and leave room for other fees or rounding. The 50-unit open must revert even if both circuits allow 3,369 bps.

## Acceptance and estimates

- At identical accrued starting state, show one successful open, a market deny when the account allows, and an account deny when the market allows. Verify matched op-6 inputs, real borrower, final principal and fees, cash, collateral shares, cleared approvals and unchanged balances after rejection.
- Open only within conservative LTV, equity, position and leverage bounds after actual fill. Exercise adverse fill/min-out, fee cap, stale or invalid feed, wrong pair, and insufficient DEX, book and flash inventories. A failed callback must leave no open position, allowance, debt or claim.
- Demonstrate a full close with both policies unavailable and new opens disabled. Measure actual flash repayment, zero debt and owner-only residual. Demonstrate owner-funded in-kind recovery with unavailable route/oracle. Demonstrate permissionless partial liquidation when past the hard threshold, no liquidation at equality, preservation of borrower surplus and no changes in another market. Show accrued debt and liquidator inventory in receipts.
- Local or testnet fixtures should use a seeded, fixed-pair mock router that transfers real mock-token inventory in both directions and enforces min-out. Use distinct mock oracle, router and asset labels in the UI, logs and manifest. The existing Fuji example is bound to chain 43113 and its Fuji tokens. Its mechanism illustrates a fixture but cannot run unchanged on X Layer. A fixture demonstrates contract behavior only. Calendar dependencies include testnet gas, organizer feedback, access to liquidation capital, independent audit and founder approval. Actual funded launch has no reliable completion estimate. A literal full-stack legacy port also adds vault accounting, fee distribution, two liquidation mechanisms and upgrade governance outside these ranges.

Keep the margin capability disabled and absent from deployed manifests until the acceptance checks pass. A labeled local mock demonstration could be considered after P0 acceptance and successful open, close and liquidation checks. A real-venue prototype additionally requires confirmed reversible route capacity at the proposed size, fresh independent price feeds for both assets, a funded flash/exit source and funded liquidators. No margin configuration here is a release parameter.
